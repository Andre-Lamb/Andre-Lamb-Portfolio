Acting Reel

Process Breakdown

Bill (Nugget)

A small role I played in the short film ‘Nugget’, Bill is an egotistical snitch who would film the lead trio nuggeting bags back in school, landing them in detention many a time. Now their new boss, Bill is oblivious to the fact that his old nemeses are in his employ. I was offered this role after the director saw me perform in a variety show, as he was looking for a villain for his character driven comedy short. This was the most professional shoot I’d been on despite everyone being volunteers, and though my overall screen time is minimal, the experience was well worth the time.

Dr. Alex Mathers, Dr. Fran Brady and Blake Collins (Stranger Things Experience)

A very eccentric set of employees from Hawkins Power and Light, each interpreted in a range of ways by the cast of Luna Park’s ‘Stranger Things Experience’. Following around a month of scattered training and rehearsal days, I brought these characters to life for a year in front of thousands of guests, helping to guide them through the rooms and setting the scenes to follow. Dr. Alex was where I spent the most time, an anxious and scatter-brained scientist who has become dreary and apathetic following years of failed, monotonous tests. Throughout up to 15 minutes, he goes from insulting the guests to unbridled excitement, finishing with pure panic and worry for what will happen to his new ‘friends’. Dr. Fran was an over-excited and slightly creepy new employee who eagerly accosted guests about their personal information, dropping hints at the deeper experiments that were taking place within the lab. Finally, Blake was the clinical and charismatic public relations officer that introduced guests to the study they had volunteered for. Through a fake smile and overwhelming information, he explained what the guests should expect, whilst showing that nothing they were told was to be believed.

James, Ted, Tony and Maxwell Brewer (The Bookstore)

A lovely and whimsical fringe show I performed in, set in a seemingly magical bookstore that propagates love and story. As an ensemble dramatic comedy, I played 4 different characters, each with vastly different personalities that I had a lot of fun embodying. The first was James, a soft-spoken author feeling down about their book signing, followed by Ted, an awkward, slightly creepy and apparently endearing customer who doesn’t know how to speak to his crush. I then transitioned into the 2 villains of the story, the first being Tony, the controlling, narcissistic and daft boyfriend of the lead Rachel, and the last being Maxwell Brewer, an out-of-touch, hyper-rich real estate magnate looking to purchase the bookstore’s land for a skyrise apartment. Each character’s descriptions were quite simple, which gave me a lot of freedom to explore my range and create incredibly unique characters that I’m sure were represented differently in other renditions of the play.

Nick Neotoma, King of the Rats (Oliver Mantle)

Everybody’s least favourite colleague, Nick Neotoma claims to be the King of the Rats (after the previous king, his Father, passed) and only ever talks about the going ons of the apparent sewer civilisation. This would be very interesting if anyone believed Nick, or simply if he wasn’t so annoying. As the webseries is still in production, I’ve only been involved in shooting a few scenes as of now, though the character of Nick has come quite naturally. A lot of this role is going on and on about rat related events, talking at other characters who begrudgingly sit within earshot. During one scene, we recorded some background dialogue, which amounted to 15 minutes of me telling another actor about how prejudices have altered the general public to think much less of rats than they deserve.

El Escorpion (Devil’s Pardon)

A vigilante turned outlaw, exiled from Backbone Valley, El Escorpion is the ever-present force that forces the events of the student play ‘Devil’s Pardon’ to take place. Seeking revenge for the cold-blooded murder of his dying ma, he haunts El Diablo, who is the town’s sheriff and El Escorpion’s old partner. This character was incredibly fun to bring to life, as he’s a very serious and angry character in an otherwise comedic play. Most of his first appearances are brief, like a shadow, so I spent a lot of time rehearsing the small movements and choices to sell that he had been planning this revenge plot for many years. This role also involved two things I hadn’t done before; stage fighting and speaking Spanish. Stage fighting came quite naturally, as I have a lot of experience with dance and I had grown a strong bond with my fight partner over the course of rehearsals. The Spanish, however, was much harder and required a few techniques to grasp. I had my director record himself saying each line so I had a reference and spent some time researching the meaning of each so I could decide on my intentions and inflections for each word.
