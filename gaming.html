Game Design Reel

Process Breakdown

BanKalogue

Made to the theme ‘Balance’ over 5 weeks with a team of 3, BanKalogue is a Victorian era banking sim in which all the bookkeeping is analogue. As in you draw everything from scratch. As the lead game designer, writer and artist, I had a lot of fun blending each aspect to create an absurdist and silly vibe to counteract the otherwise bleak environment. Featuring mechanics such as randomised customers, drawing without erasing, Victorian currency lingo and a grading system that can fire useless employees. Try it here!

Wake ‘N’ ‘Grade

Made to the theme ‘The Wake’ over roughly 3 days with a team of 2, Wake ‘N’ ‘Grade is an arcade style boating game in which the player must rescue wake boarders, wake the local fishermen and upgrade your boat as much as possible before you run out of time. As the lead artist and audio designer, and co-game designer, we spent a lot of our time going through different ideas and potential mechanics. While we were initially going to make a story-based horror game, a few of the guidelines of the gamejam caused us to pivot radically to the gnarly and almost neon aesthetic of Wake ‘N’ ‘Grade. Due to limitations of free time from both team members, we were forced to cut a lot of corners in both coding and art, though I’m very proud of the final product, which came 4th in the people’s choice category. Try it here!

Visions of Home and Self

Made to the theme ‘VHS’ over 2.5 weeks solo, Visions of Home and Self is a horror themed narrative and puzzle walking simulator in which the player must remember where they parked their car in an infinite and liminal parking lot. This was the first game I created entirely alone, and was a great test of the game asset, game design, audio and coding skills I’d developed over all my group projects. I’m most proud of the procedural parking lot generation I created, which seamlessly spawns and culls modular tiles just outside of the player’s view range. This optimisation allowed the game to run smoothly in web, whilst also making the player feel truly lost, as tiles they had previously seen are replaced when they return. Whilst I’m very happy with how the environment and story came out, I do think some of the puzzles were a bit too obscure, which prevented a few people from finishing the game with the good ending. Despite that, the game placed 3rd overall in the gamejam, which I’m thoroughly pleased with. Try it here!

Trainsformer: The Western Track

Made to the theme ‘Train’ in just 2 weeks with a team of 3, Trainsformer is a western themed turn-based RPG in which an ever-transforming train fights some ever-evolving robots. As the lead game designer and artist, I spent a lot of time designing, modelling and rigging the ridiculously overengineered train in conjunction with the complex type chart and move effects, all tied together with the western theme. This resulted in moves such as ‘loco motion,’ a whiskey type buff, or ‘6 tooter,’ a steam type attack. Each move originally had more in-depth effects, with both a passive that remained active whilst equipped, and an active for when the move was swapped out, though they had to be reigned back to better fit time constraints. While incomplete, the absolute style this project oozes makes up for the lack of clarity in my eyes. Try it here!

Tavern of Fates

Made to the theme ‘Fate’ in just 2 weeks with a team of 3, Tavern of Fates is a narrative roguelike card game in which you prophesy your fate and play event cards in order to push your reality towards that fate. With both the fate and event cards based on a full tarot deck, I spent hours researching each card and its meaning to create little stories that corresponded to both its upright and reversed form. Additionally, each story needed to clearly align with the stats that the card would change, which created a very difficult writing challenge that I am very proud of completing. Due to the tight timeframe, the fate card icons weren’t able to be created, and the UI is quite messy. If I were to reapproach this project, I’d work on making every bit of UI diegetic, as objects on the table, and fix up the dice system to limit the number of nodes the player can pick from. Try it here!

Remedium Animi

The first 3D game I made, alongside 3 other students, I was the lead game designer, artist and also coded the majority of the combat/game mechanics. A twin-stick puzzle and action game in which a crazed medieval plague doctor uses various techniques to cure deadly diseases. I did a lot of research on real world cures and aesthetics surrounding plague doctors in order to create 4 unique abilities to use in curing. My biggest struggle was creating an outline shader on the 3D objects, due to the isometric angle. The orthographic camera caused issues with how it rendered the vertex offset, creating the outlines far away from where they were intended to be. Due to my limited coding ability at the time and my already heavy workload, I unfortunately had to scrap the idea. Despite its problems and bugs, I believe this is a strong project, and one I will be revisiting in future. Try it here!
